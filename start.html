step 1 · 2 min

Get online

You need a phone or a computer, and internet. A phone is totally fine. Free Wi‑Fi is easier to find than you think:

📚 Public library

Free Wi‑Fi and free computers. You can usually get a library card with no fee. Staff will help you log on.

🏠 Shelters & centers

Many shelters, job centers and community centers have Wi‑Fi or a computer room. Just ask the front desk.

☕ Coffee & fast food

Most have free Wi‑Fi. Look for a network with the store's name.

📱 Cheap phone plans

In the US, the Lifeline program can give you a phone plan for little or nothing if you get benefits. Search “Lifeline phone” or ask a caseworker.

step 2 · 10 min

Make a free email

An email address is your key to almost everything online — jobs, benefits, AI apps. We'll use Gmail. It's free.

  1. Go to accounts.google.com/signup. On a phone, you can also open the Gmail app and tap Create account.
  2. Type your first and last name. Then your birthday.
  3. Pick your email address. Google will suggest some, or make your own. Need ideas? Use the tool below.
  4. Make a password. Long is better than weird. (Ideas below.)
  5. Google might ask for a phone number to send you a code. If you don't have one, see the tip below.
  6. Skip anything that says “optional.” Agree to the terms. You're in.

Screens change sometimes, so yours may look a little different. That's normal — just keep going.

step 4 · 2 min

Send your first message

Talk to it like you'd text a smart friend. No special words needed. Copy this, paste it in the message box, hit send:

your first prompt 
Hi. I'm new to this. Explain what you can help me with in 5 short bullet points. Use simple words.

Then try something real. Here's one that works for almost anything:

let it interview you 
Ask me questions one at a time until you understand what I need. Then help me with it.

What I need help with: [DESCRIBE IT IN A FEW WORDS]
How to paste:

On a phone: press and hold in the message box → tap Paste. On a computer: Ctrl + V (or ⌘ + V on a Mac).

step 5 · 1 min

Save the good stuff

Phones get lost. Chats get buried. Here's the simplest trick: email it to yourself. Your email lives online, so you can open it from any library computer, any phone, any time.

  1. When the AI writes something good (a resume, a letter), ask: “Write this in a code block for me.”
  2. Tap the copy button on the box.
  3. Open your email → Compose → put your own address in “To” → paste → send.
  4. Give it a clear subject like RESUME - Oct 2026 so you can search for it later.

step 6 · 1 min

Stay safe

AI is a tool, not a person. It's really helpful — and sometimes it's wrong.

✓ Do
  • Double-check anything about law, health, money or deadlines with a real person.
  • Call before you go — addresses and hours can be out of date.
  • Ask “Are you sure? What might be wrong?”
  • Log out when you use a shared computer.
✕ Don't
  • Type your Social Security number, bank or card numbers, or passwords.
  • Pay anyone who says they'll “unlock” AI for you. The free version is enough.
  • Trust links or phone numbers without checking them.
  • Let it speak for you without reading it first.
